About

Polymath from the Niger Delta

Ikanke-Abasi Umana is a programs and operations professional, researcher, writer, and event host based in Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria. She studied Communication Arts at the University of Uyo.

She currently serves as Product Integration & Growth Manager and TalentWorks Venture Lead at Mosaic Human Capital Innovations (MHCI), building operational systems, leading product-market strategy, running the TalentWorks talent placement venture, designing training curricula, and managing multi-stakeholder partnerships. This year that includes leading the TechRise Women Initiative and Akwa Ibom Tech Week 2026. She is also Co-Founder of Founder Advocacy Network (FAN).

She has been hosting events since she was a child: children’s day programmes and teens conventions at her parents’ church, Model UN director in secondary school, to tech festival stages and founder meetups. Her range is wide by design. When something needs doing, she steps forward.

The Work

Four rooms, one address.

By day she runs talent programmes, builds learner portals, leads product-market strategy, and coordinates government and NGO partnerships across a multi-venture consultancy. By evening she chairs founder meetups, writes essays, and designs event collateral. The scope is who she is.

01

Programs & Operations

At MHCI, she built TalentWorks from an idea into a working placement venture, and now looks after a growing roster of more than seventeen executive assistants placed with clients in Abuja, Uyo, Enugu and beyond. She designed the TechRise Women Initiative for 250 women with the Office of the First Lady (Akwa Ibom State) and AfriLabs, negotiated the agreement between the three, and now leads its delivery through three partner organisations. When a process keeps tripping people up, she tends to build the tool that fixes it. Her builds have a room of their own. At Zitere Social Enterprises (Triift Africa), she managed the HOME Project in Ogun State. She is Co-Founder of Founder Advocacy Network (FAN).

Research & Analysis

Her undergraduate seminar research on gender representation in UNICEF’s X posts is in the process of being published in an academic journal, co-authored with her university lecturer. The study found stories centring boys were systematically underrepresented. Her final-year thesis studied how ThisDay and The Punch covered Nigeria’s 2023 fuel subsidy removal. She also writes analytical memos connecting policy and economic data to organisational decisions.

The Builds

Things she made because the work needed them.

She has no formal training in code. She taught herself to build because the manual version of the work kept letting people down, and nobody asked her to. Each of these began as a problem someone on the team kept running into.

01

AccessTrack

The ESP learner portal

One place for a seven-week training programme. Learners find their sessions, assignments and progress there, and the people running it grade work, issue certificates and report on each cohort. It took over from a tangle of spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads and email, and now belongs to the programme’s Training Lead.

02

Scout

For MHCI’s ventures

Finds funding opportunities and scores how well each one fits the studio’s ventures, so the team spends its time on the applications worth writing.

03

MHCI Brain

For the whole studio

A shared home for how the studio works, with maps of the organisation, its procedures and its contacts. One corner, Grapevine, keeps the informal stories that never survive in a procedure. She thinks of it as closer to a zine than a wiki.

04

TalentWorks Portal

For TalentWorks

Follows every executive assistant from the roster through to a placement, and helps match each client’s brief to the right person.

05

Brief

For the founder

Sorts the founder’s inbox by urgency and sends one summary each morning.

Also on the bench: a candidate dossier generator for TalentWorks, the application and screening system for TechRise Women, and the automation behind her weekly reports.

Undergraduate thesis · 2024

Coverage of Nigeria’s Fuel Subsidy Removal by Select Nigerian Newspapers

Content analysis of 79 editions each from ThisDay and The Punch. Found initial high-frequency coverage declining over six months, with framing favouring economic impacts and government perspectives over public voice. Recommended consistent coverage and diverse viewpoints.
